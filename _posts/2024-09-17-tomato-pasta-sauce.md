# Tomato Pasta Sauce with Roasted Peppers

A simple, flexible tomato sauce great for pasta, incorporating sautéed onions, garlic, and optional roasted peppers.

## Ingredients

- 1–2 tbsp olive oil
- 1/4 yellow onion, finely chopped
- 2 cloves garlic, minced
- 1 can (28 oz) whole peeled tomatoes or San Marzano tomatoes
- Salt, to taste
- Freshly ground black pepper, to taste
- 1/2 tsp dried oregano or Italian seasoning (optional)
- Roasted red peppers, chopped (optional, for extra sweetness and texture)
- Red pepper flakes (optional, for heat)
- Fresh basil or parsley (optional, added at the end)

## Instructions

1. **Sauté aromatics**: Heat olive oil in a pan over medium heat. Add chopped onion and cook until translucent (4–5 minutes). Add garlic and cook another 30–60 seconds until fragrant.
2. **Add tomatoes**: Pour in the tomatoes with their juice. Use a wooden spoon to break them up a bit.
3. **Season**: Add salt, pepper, and optional herbs or chili flakes. Stir to combine.
4. **Simmer**: Reduce heat and let sauce simmer for at least 20–30 minutes, stirring occasionally. For deeper flavor, simmer up to an hour.
5. **Add roasted peppers**: Stir in roasted red peppers near the end of cooking if using. You can leave them chunky or blend for a smoother sauce.
6. **Blend (optional)**: Use an immersion blender if a smooth sauce is desired.
7. **Finish**: Stir in fresh basil or parsley right before serving.

## Notes

- **Roasted Peppers**: User asked about adding roasted peppers to the sauce. Adding them at the end is ideal to retain their texture and flavor. They can be blended for smoothness or left chunky.
- **Garlic & Onion Quantities**: User used 1/4 of a yellow onion and 2 garlic cloves, which works well for a small batch.
- **Tomato Choice**: San Marzano tomatoes were used along with diced tomatoes for balanced flavor and texture.
- **Simmering Time**: Letting the sauce simmer for 20–30 minutes is sufficient, but it can be simmered longer for a richer flavor.
- **Storage**: Sauce can be cooled and stored in the refrigerator or freezer. Let it cool fully before transferring to containers.
